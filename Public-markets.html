Jean-Talon

Jean-Talon is so much larger than life that I spent several months almost outright refusing to go, decrying it as a tourist trap, not the real Montréal, and overpriced. Sure, there are plenty of tourists, the vibe of the market is quite different from those in most everyday neighbourhoods, and some distributors sell non-local products at hefty markups. But, after putting this stubborn notion aside and learning to read the market as just another element in the fabric of the city, I looked past the $12 juice and suspiciously plentiful mangoes to the seasonal rhythm of berries and root vegetables, steady cadence of locally greenhouse-grown cucumbers and tomatoes, and infectious curiousity of the market. These days, I go maybe once every month or two, sometimes to buy produce, and sometimes just to look around.

This has helped me appreciate the underlying benevolence of the market—for example, once, early in my quest to speak as little English as possible in public (unless the person I'm speaking to leads in English, I'm in a group where everyone else is speaking English, I'm visiting a regional establishment where the most accessible language of the English-French dichotomy is English, or I strand myself beyond my French abilities), I was trying to buy some limes. The kiosk had a $5 Interac minimum and I didn't have the correct change, but the proprietor let me keep the limes for five cents under the nominal price, saying something about how I could pay properly next time. I thanked the proprietor profusely, but, while I was walking away, I couldn't shake the impression that this would not have happened if I had been yet another strict anglophone infringing the fundamental francosphere-awareness of this city.

Atwater

Similarly flashy, busy, and laid out similarly to Jean-Talon with a dedicated food court, greengrocery section, and indoor shop section, Marché Atwater is nevertheless a bit calmer than its more famous counterpart.

Jean-Brillant

Even more lowkey than Marché Atwater, I get the sense that Marché Jean-Brillant is even further off the typical tourist path than Jean-Talon and Atwater. One clear advantage Jean-Brillant has over its fellow markets its opening hours: 24/7, from April to November! They sometimes sell containers of berries for 99 cents during the peaks of their respective seasons!

Maisonneuve

Similarly lowkey and local-feeling to Jean-Brillant, but I don't get the impression that some of the perishables move through their inventory very quickly.
