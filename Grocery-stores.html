  • Grocery stores by type
    • local
      • Marché Newon: wide variety of produce and spices
      • Marché El-Nahrawy: nice preserves and condiments
      • Marché Mizan: amazing selection of locally baked breads that are intermittently in stock, like injera, Afghan bread, manakich, etc. Best price I've seen in town for large bottles of flower essence waters. Around Ramadan, they stock an impressive variety of dates and the Jordanian ones are particularly good. But, the produce tends to be less consistent.
      • Marché Inter Asia: nice selection of dried peppers and frozen West African greens
      • Marché Branche d'Olivier: cheap lime powder
      • Fruiterie Vert Pomme: super fresh, wide selection, and good prices
      • Marché Saluk: non-industrial sangak, non e tanuri, etc. Really nice prepared foods (especially the kofte tabrizi and chicken tagine), family business, and sometimes slightly past-date products are available for free outside
      • Marché Andes: nice giant corn
      • Supermarché International: Western African tisanes and spices, broad range of Indian Subcontinent products. Produce is less consistent, and some inventory doesn't seem to move too quickly.
      • Marché BKR: best prices on olive oil, dried fruit, and pomegranate molasses I've seen anywhere in MTL. Produce is inconsistent
      • Épicerie du Bazaar: nice preserved and pickled vegetables
      • Euro Victoria: Caucasus, Slavic language sphere, Baltics, Balkans. Central Asia is really the only FSU region not super well represented here
      • Marché Kim Phat: really nice variety of greens, but all sections are great
      • Marché Tharsini: probably the most comprehensive South Asian grocery store I've been to in Montreal, and I've been to probably at least 10-20. If Google Maps gets confused about which one I mean, I'm talking about the one a fraction of a block south of Côte-Sainte-Catherine in the southwest corner of the intersection of CSC and Victoria—right by the eponymous metro station
      • Deli Queen (деликатесы) near Snowdon. It has slightly nicer vibes than Euro Victoria, in my experience
      • Supermarché Byblos: best prices on 100% sesame tahini I've seen anywhere in town
      • Fruiterie Shaana: wide variety of Antillean and Central American condiments, cheap halva
    • chain
      • Maxi
        • It's Loblaw's answer to the Super C price tier, but there are dedicated South Asian, East Asian, Antillean, kosher, halal, Middle Eastern, African, and "general interest" sections.
        • The completeness of each of these cultural sections varies a lot depending on which neighbourhood you're in
        • Maxi&Cie always has a broader selection than Maxi
        • Less-central locations generally have more selection (unless you're in a predominantly Franco-Canadian area), but the &Cie effect is more significant
        • E.g. in my opinion: CDN Maxi&Cie > Parc-Ex Maxi&Cie > Plateau/Saint-Urbain Maxi > Centre-Ville/René-Lévesque Maxi ~ Préfontaine Maxi. I sometimes go out of my way to go to locations I mentally rank higher so I can get cheaper "club size" products like oats and frozen fruit
      • Super C (good prices, but limited selection)
      • T&T
        • Amazing produce selection (variety and freshness)
        • Soy drinks
        • In-store cafes and free samples
        • Baked goods
        • Prepared foods
